## 3. Branch-Level Observations

### Branch C — Mumbai (Top Branch)

- **Highest total sales revenue** in the dataset.
- Mumbai is India's largest commercial city — higher purchasing power per customer contributes to larger basket sizes.
- Cheese and Paneer transactions are most concentrated at Branch C.
- Branch C records some of the highest single-transaction values (e.g., 6× Paneer at ₹1,200).
- UPI is the dominant payment method at Branch C.

### Branch A — Delhi (Second)

- Strong Member customer base.
- Cheese and Coffee are the most purchased products.
- ATV is competitive with Branch C.
- Credit Card usage is proportionally higher here compared to the other branches.

### Branch B — Bangalore (Third)

- Slightly behind Branch A and Branch C in total revenue.
- Bangalore's tech-savvy demographic drives higher UPI usage.
- Coffee and Juice are particularly popular — consistent with Bangalore's café culture.
- Oats and Yogurt show higher purchase frequency compared to Delhi and Mumbai, reflecting health-conscious consumer behaviour.

---

## 6. Business Implications

| Branch | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Branch C (Mumbai) | Maintain premium product availability (Cheese, Paneer); target upselling on large quantity orders |
| Branch A (Delhi) | Increase UPI adoption incentives; leverage higher Credit Card users for premium product promotions |
| Branch B (Bangalore) | Expand health-focused product range (Oats, Yogurt); leverage Coffee popularity for combo promotions |

### General Branch Strategy

1. **Even revenue contribution from all three branches** is a positive indicator — no single branch is carrying disproportionate load.
2. **Standardise top-performing product stocking** (Cheese, Paneer, Eggs) across all three branches to prevent localised stockouts.
3. **Localise promotions**: Mumbai (premium, high-ATV), Delhi (membership-driven), Bangalore (health + digital-first).
